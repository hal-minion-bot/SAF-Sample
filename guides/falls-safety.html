Falls Prevention & Home Safety

Why falls matter, a room-by-room walkthrough, the fixes worth doing first, and what to do right after a fall.

Why falls matter

A fall is the single most common reason an older adult ends up in hospital — and one fall often leads to a fear of falling that shrinks someone's world. The good news is that most falls happen for a handful of predictable reasons, and most of those are fixable in an afternoon. You don't need to renovate; you need to remove the few real hazards.

A room-by-room walkthrough

Walk the home the way your loved one moves through it. In the bathroom, look at getting on and off the toilet and in and out of the tub — these are the riskiest moments. In the bedroom, check the path to the bathroom at night and whether a light is within reach. In hallways and stairs, look for loose rugs, clutter, and missing or wobbly handrails. In the kitchen, notice anything stored too high or too low that invites reaching or bending.

The fixes that matter most

A few changes do most of the work: install grab bars by the toilet and in the tub (not towel bars — proper anchored grab bars), remove or tape down loose rugs, add a night light along the path to the bathroom, and make sure every stairway has a sturdy handrail. Good footwear with non-slip soles matters more than people expect. If reaching is a problem, move daily items to waist height so nothing requires a step stool.

What to do right after a fall

If someone falls, don't rush to lift them. First check for pain, especially in the hip, head, or back, and ask if they hit their head. If there's severe pain, bleeding, confusion, or they can't get up, call 911. If they seem fine, help them rise slowly — roll to their side, get to hands and knees, and use a stable chair to push up. Afterward, tell their doctor: a fall is worth a check-up even when nothing seems broken, because it can signal a treatable cause.
