Hospital to Home: The First Days

The days right after a discharge are the riskiest. Here's how to make them safe and calm.

Before discharge

Don't leave the hospital without the basics in hand. Ask for the discharge summary, a clear medication list (including anything stopped or changed), and the date of the first follow-up appointment. Ask plainly: what should we watch for, what's normal, and what counts as an emergency? If new equipment or home care is needed, confirm it's arranged before you go — not "in progress."

The first 48 hours

This window is where problems show up. Fill any new prescriptions the same day and set up a simple medication schedule so nothing is missed or doubled. Keep the first day quiet — rest, fluids, light meals. Make sure someone can check in, and that the path to the bathroom is clear and lit. Write down any questions as they come up so you're ready for the follow-up call or visit.

The first two weeks

Recovery is rarely a straight line. Keep every follow-up appointment, and don't skip them just because things seem fine. Watch energy, appetite, and mood as much as the physical wound or illness. If home care or therapy was arranged, make sure visits are actually happening and speak up if they're not. Keep that notebook of names and numbers close — you may need to chase something down.

Warning signs to watch

Call for help if you see new or worsening shortness of breath, chest pain, a fever, confusion, a wound that's red, swollen, or draining, or a sudden drop in alertness or ability to stay steady. New severe pain or an inability to keep food and fluids down also warrants a call. When in doubt, it's always better to ask than to wait.

Who to call

For an emergency — trouble breathing, chest pain, a serious fall, sudden confusion — call 911. For questions that aren't emergencies, call your provincial health line (often 811) or the family doctor's office. If home care was arranged, keep the coordinator's number handy. Knowing in advance who to call for what takes the panic out of the moment you actually need it.
